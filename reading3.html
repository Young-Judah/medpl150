Nevaeh Rochford's Novel Reflection ♡

Reading Memunatu: The Distant Patient, especially the chapter “Birth in My Village,” felt less like reading a traditional story and more like being placed inside someone else’s reality. What stood out to me most was not just the content itself, but how the format shaped my emotional response. The combination of visuals, short text, and “swiping” created a very immersive experience that made it hard to separate myself as just a reader. It felt intentional, like the design was meant to slow me down and make me sit with what I was seeing instead of just moving on quickly.
One of the biggest things I took away from it is how unequal access to healthcare can completely change what should be a natural life experience. It’s easy to forget, especially living in a place where hospitals and emergency care are accessible, that childbirth can still be extremely dangerous in other parts of the world. The story made that gap feel very real without needing to over-explain it. It also made me think about how often we overlook these realities because they are not part of our daily lives.
I also found myself thinking a lot about the way the story was presented online. The scrolling effect reminded me of how we normally consume content on our phones, especially when we are swiping through social media carousels or news without fully processing everything. But in this case, the design forced a different kind of attention. Instead of quickly moving past difficult images or ideas, I felt like I had to pause and actually absorb them. It made me realize how powerful digital storytelling can be when it is designed with intention.
At the same time, it made me reflect on my own habits as someone who constantly consumes information through a screen. We are so used to seeing serious global issues come through in short, fragmented pieces online that we sometimes become emotionally detached. This graphic novel uses that same scrolling behavior but turns it into something more meaningful, almost challenging the reader to stay present instead of disengaging.
Overall, my reaction to Memunatu is less about the specific details of the story and more about how it made me feel while experiencing it. It showed me how design and storytelling can shape empathy in a digital space. It also made me more aware of how easily we can overlook real human experiences when they are filtered through screens and constant information flow.
