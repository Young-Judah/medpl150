Nevaeh Rochford's Nuclear Reflection ♡

W.J. Hennigan’s article was honestly very eye-opening and unsettling to read because it made nuclear war feel much more real and current instead of something that only happened during the Cold War. Before reading this, I knew nuclear weapons were dangerous, but I did not realize how seriously governments are preparing for the possibility of a nuclear attack in Ukraine. The details about hospitals preparing for radiation victims and emergency nuclear response plans made the situation feel very real and scary.
What stood out to me the most was how the article described the aftermath of even a small tactical nuclear weapon. The descriptions of radiation sickness, environmental destruction, and long-term effects on people and ecosystems really showed how devastating nuclear weapons would be, even on a smaller scale. It also made me realize how fragile peace between countries can be and how quickly conflict could escalate if one nation decides to use nuclear weapons. At the same time, the article also made me think about how fragile global peace really is. A major point in the article is that nuclear deterrence has worked for decades because countries feared mutual destruction. However, if one country actually decides to use a nuclear weapon, even on a smaller scale, that balance could completely collapse. That possibility is frightening because once escalation begins, there is no guarantee it can be controlled. The article emphasizes how close tensions became in 2022 when intelligence agencies believed there was a serious possibility Russia could use a tactical nuclear weapon in Ukraine. Knowing that world leaders were privately preparing for this while many ordinary citizens barely knew about it is unsettling.
Another point I found important was the discussion about failing treaties and lack of communication between nuclear powers. The article explains that many Cold War agreements that once limited nuclear weapons are disappearing, and that the New START treaty may expire soon. I agree with the author that transparency and communication are extremely important when dealing with weapons this destructive. Without agreements and diplomacy, misunderstandings and fear can escalate conflicts very quickly.
I also really liked the art style and scrolling effect of the website itself. The way the article was designed almost felt immersive, and the scrolling reminded me of how we constantly consume media and world tragedies through our phones every day. As I kept scrolling, it felt like the article was intentionally imitating the nonstop flow of information people see on social media. I think that design choice made the article even more impactful because it connected modern media consumption with such a serious global issue. Overall, I think the article was very effective because it combined detailed information with emotional storytelling and visuals that kept the reader engaged while also making the dangers of nuclear conflict feel more personal and real.
